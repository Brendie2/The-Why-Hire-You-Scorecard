You’re good at the work. But is it clear why someone should pay you?

Being good at what you do is important. But people can’t make decisions based on expertise they can’t see, understand, or connect to a problem they need solved. And that creates some frustrating situations.

You have several skills, but you don’t know what to sell.You can do a lot of things well. The problem is figuring out which service to offer, who needs it, and how to explain its value.
People see your profile, but don’t know what to hire you for.You know what you’re capable of. Someone discovering you for the first time may not understand it from your headline, About section, or content.
You keep posting, but you’re not getting the conversations you want.You’re sharing ideas and trying to stay consistent. But you’re not sure whether your content attracts the right people, communicates your value, or gives them a clear next step.
You want to make money from your personal brand, but you’re not sure how.You understand that visibility matters. You’re still figuring out how to connect that visibility to a service people can actually buy.

These problems can look similar. They don’t always have the same cause.

That’s why the next step isn’t automatically to post more, redesign your profile, lower your price, or buy another course. First, you need a better idea of what deserves your attention.

What the Scorecard looks at

Find out where you may be getting stuck.

The free Why-Hire-You Scorecard helps you assess six areas that influence how clearly your expertise translates into a service the right people can understand and consider buying.

01Do you know who you’re trying to help?

It’s difficult to explain why someone should hire you if you’re still trying to figure out who your service is for and what problem it solves.

02Is your offer clear?

Can someone understand what you do, what they can hire you for, and what they can expect from working with you?

03Is it clear why someone should choose you?

You don’t have to be the only person offering a service. But people need a reason to understand how your approach or expertise is relevant to them.

04Do people have reasons to trust what you say?

It’s one thing to say you’re good at what you do. It’s another to give people useful evidence that helps them evaluate your expertise.

05Are people beginning to associate you with something specific?

If someone comes across your profile and content, can they understand what you want to be known for, or does everything point in a different direction?

06Is the next step clear?

When someone becomes interested in your work, do they know what to do next? Can they easily understand how to enquire, evaluate your service, or take the next step?

The Scorecard helps you examine these areas without assuming that every one of them is a problem for you.

Who is this for?

The Scorecard is for you if…

  • You have professional skills or experience you want to earn money from.
  • You’re trying to figure out what service to sell.
  • You’re already offering a service but struggling to explain why people should pay for it.
  • You’re using LinkedIn or building a personal brand and want it to support your business.
  • You’re creating content but aren’t sure whether it’s helping the right people understand your value.
  • You’ve tried different strategies and want to work out what deserves your attention first.

You don’t need thousands of followers. You don’t need a perfect LinkedIn profile. And you don’t need to have everything figured out before you begin.

You just need a genuine desire to understand how you’re presenting your expertise and what you could improve.

What this won’t do

Let’s be clear.

The Scorecard won’t guarantee clients, sales, or income. It won’t replace conversations with potential buyers or tell you with certainty why someone didn’t buy. And it won’t magically fix your offer, your content, or your business in five minutes.

What it can do is give you a useful starting point, help you notice possible gaps, and give you a more deliberate way to decide what to examine next.

Because the last thing you need is another list of things to change without knowing why you’re changing them.
