You can be good at your work and still be difficult to hire.

Your skills are what you bring. Your offer is the specific problem you choose to solve for a buyer. People can’t decide based on skill they can’t see, or connect to a problem they have. Often they pick someone cheaper or with less experience because it feels safer, not because that person is better.

You have several skills, and choosing one feels like losing the rest.Often it isn’t more skills you need. It’s deciding which one you’re selling first, who it’s for, and how to explain it.
People see your profile, but can’t say what to hire you for.You know what you’re capable of. Someone discovering you for the first time may not understand it from your headline, About section, or content.
You keep posting, but nobody asks how to work with you.A post is not an offer. Likes are not the goal. Conversations are. Your content may not be telling the right people what to do next.
You want to make money from your personal brand, and saying your price feels hard.Visibility matters, but it is not a business. And when it’s time to name a price, you may feel like a fraud, or hide behind your hours.

These problems can look similar. They don’t always have the same cause.

A prospect saying “too expensive”, or going silent, doesn’t tell you enough to change your price or your offer. First you need a better idea of what deserves your attention.

What the Scorecard looks at

Find out where you may be getting stuck.

The free Why-Hire-You Scorecard looks at six areas that decide whether people understand what you sell, trust you, and say yes.

01Do you know who you’re trying to help?

It’s difficult to explain why someone should hire you if you’re still trying to figure out who your service is for and what problem it solves.

02Have you chosen what you’re selling, and what it costs?

Can a stranger tell what you do, what they get, and what it costs? A list of skills is not an offer, and neither is an offer you’re afraid to commit to.

03Is it clear why they should pick you, and can you say it plainly?

You don’t have to be the only one who does what you do. People need a reason that matters to them, and you need to be able to say it without flinching.

04Is there something that makes it feel safe to pay you?

Saying you’re good is one thing. Giving a careful buyer something to point to is another.

05Do your profile, posts and offer say the same thing?

If someone followed you for a month, would they know what you want to be known for, or does everything point a different way?

06What happens when someone is interested?

Do they know the next step? Does it turn into a conversation, or do they go quiet after your price?

The Scorecard helps you examine these areas without assuming that every one of them is a problem for you.

Who is this for?

Is the Scorecard right for you?

It’s for you if…

  • You have skills, but you’re not sure what exactly you can sell, or which one to lead with.
  • You already offer a service, but clients are inconsistent.
  • You post on LinkedIn, but nobody asks how to work with you.
  • People show interest, then go quiet after your price or proposal.
  • You see people with less experience getting chosen, and you want to know why.
  • You’ve tried a lot of things and want to know what to fix first.

It’s probably not for you if…

  • You only want followers or viral reach.
  • You want someone to run your LinkedIn or ghostwrite for you.
  • You want a done-for-you business, website or funnel.
  • You want guaranteed clients without doing the work.
  • You don’t have a skill you could deliver to a client yet. If that’s you, the Scorecard will tell you honestly what to do first.

You don’t need thousands of followers. You don’t need a perfect LinkedIn profile. And you don’t need to have everything figured out before you begin.

You just need to be willing to see what a stranger sees.

What this won’t do

Let’s be clear.

The Scorecard won’t guarantee clients, sales, or income. It won’t replace conversations with potential buyers or tell you with certainty why someone didn’t buy. And it won’t magically fix your offer, your content, or your business in six minutes.

What it can do is give you a useful starting point, help you notice possible gaps, and give you a more deliberate way to decide what to examine next.

Because the last thing you need is another list of things to change without knowing why you’re changing them.

Questions

Before you start.

Is the Scorecard really free?

Yes. You can complete the Scorecard and receive your results without paying for the assessment.

How long does it take?

About six minutes to answer 21 questions. The first asks which situation sounds most like you, 18 look at the six areas, and the last two ask whether you could deliver this now and what kind of help you want.

Do I need to have an established business?

No. You might be working with clients already, trying to package your skills into your first service, or figuring out how to make your personal brand commercially useful.

What if I’m not ready to sell yet?

Then the Scorecard will say so, and tell you what to build first. It won’t try to sell you something you aren’t ready for.

What if I don’t know the answers to some questions?

Answer as honestly as you can based on your current situation. Uncertainty can itself show you which areas deserve more thought.

Will my result tell me exactly why I’m not getting clients?

Not with certainty. Your score reflects your answers, not independently verified buyer opinions. It can suggest areas to investigate, but understanding what is actually happening may require reviewing your materials and gathering evidence from the market.

Why does it ask what kind of help I want?

So the next step fits you. If you’d rather work on it yourself, your results lead with free steps. If you want a conversation or guided help, you’ll see those options.

Will it tell me what to charge?

No. It won’t give you a number. It shows whether your offer is clear enough, and your price grounded enough, that you can name it with confidence.

Do I have to buy anything afterward?

No. The Scorecard is a free resource. If you later want help, you can book a Clarity Call, a $27 one-on-one call. Any further support is optional.
